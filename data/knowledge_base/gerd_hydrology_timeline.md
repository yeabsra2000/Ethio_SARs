# GERD Hydrology Timeline: Filling the Blue Nile Reservoir

## Location and Setting

The Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam (GERD), whose reservoir is also called Lake
Nigat, is located on the Blue Nile (Abay River) in the Benishangul-Gumuz region
of northwestern Ethiopia. The dam site lies near 11.215 degrees north latitude
and 35.093 degrees east longitude, close to the border with Sudan. The Blue Nile
gorge here is steep and narrow, which is why the site was chosen for a large
hydropower project. Downstream, the river continues into Sudan and eventually
joins the White Nile at Khartoum.

## Reservoir Scale

At full supply level the GERD reservoir is designed to hold roughly 74 billion
cubic meters (74 km3) of water. The surface area at full supply is approximately
1,874 square kilometers. For comparison, the pre-dam Blue Nile channel through
the gorge covered only a few tens of square kilometers, so filling transforms a
narrow river corridor into a large, elongated lake that extends many kilometers
upstream and into tributary valleys. This expansion is directly visible in SAR
imagery as a dark, low-backscatter water body surrounded by brighter terrain.

## Filling Milestones

The first impoundment began in 2020, when Ethiopia held back water during the
Kiremt rainy season despite ongoing negotiations. Additional filling phases
followed in 2021, 2022, and 2023. Each phase increased both the stored volume and
the surface area, and satellite radar images show the dark water signature
spreading progressively upstream. By the mid-2020s the reservoir had reached a
substantial portion of its design capacity, and further filling continued toward
the full supply level. The dam's installed hydropower capacity is designed to
reach several gigawatts once all turbines are commissioned.

## Hydrology and Downstream Context

The Blue Nile is highly seasonal. Most of its flow arrives during the Kiremt
rains, so reservoir operations aim to store peak-season water and release it
during the drier months. Filling reduces downstream flow during impoundment
years, which is why the timeline of fill phases matters for Sudan and Egypt.
Evaporation from the large reservoir surface also removes water from the system;
in a hot, arid region, evaporation losses can be significant over a year.

## Reading the SAR Timeline

In the pre-filling baseline, the gorge shows a narrow river channel with high
backscatter from rock, soil, and vegetation. During the first filling phase,
dark patches appear near the dam and spread upstream. In later years the water
becomes a large contiguous black region because calm water reflects radar away
from the sensor. The brightest features are the dam wall, access roads, and
steep rocky banks, which produce strong double-bounce or rough-surface returns.
Comparing scenes from 2019 through 2025 therefore provides a clear, cloud-free
record of one of the largest reservoir filling events in Africa.
